AIRPORT GAME
PLAYER MANUAL
Early Access – implemented mechanics

This manual explains the gameplay systems currently implemented in the project.
The game is still in development. Some systems are more complete than others.
Values described as defaults can be changed during development and balancing.

============================================================
1. GAME TIME
============================================================

The airport runs on an adjustable game clock.
You can pause the simulation.
You can run it at normal speed, 10x, 100x or 1,000x speed.

Most aircraft schedules, visitor needs, waiting times and service tasks use game time.
Higher simulation speed makes these systems progress faster.
Pausing stops game-time-based processes.

============================================================
2. BUILDING YOUR AIRPORT
============================================================

The airport is built on a grid.
Most buildings occupy one or more grid cells.
Some objects are placed along walls.
Other objects can be placed on top of supporting furniture.

You can place, rotate, move and delete buildings.
The build menu groups objects by category.
Each buildable has its own construction cost.
A cost of zero means the object is free.

You can create foundations and tarmac areas.
Grass, foundation and tarmac are walkable by pedestrians.
Runways and taxiways are not pedestrian walkways.
Buildings marked as pathfinding obstacles block pedestrian movement.
Walls block movement across their edge.
Doors allow pedestrians to pass through that edge.

You can build walls and place doors in them.
Enclosed foundation areas are detected as rooms.
A room needs walls or doors along its complete outer boundary.
A door is required for the room purposes that need an entrance.

Roads connect to neighbouring road tiles.
The road system automatically creates straight sections, corners and junctions.
Airport service vehicles use the road network to travel around the airport.

Taxiways connect runways to aircraft parking and gates.
Taxiway placement has width and surface requirements.
Aircraft use the taxiway network to find routes.
The route finder prefers suitable, direct routes.
Aircraft slow down and follow smooth turns.

Runways are used for landing and takeoff.
Runway and taxiway placement registers the ground-movement area.
Aircraft cannot use a runway or taxiway that does not provide a valid route.

You can apply floor finishes to foundation cells.
You can apply wall finishes to the inner and outer faces of walls.
Finishes change the appearance of the airport.
They do not currently change room quality or visitor needs.

You can connect fuel pipes to fuel-related buildings.
Connected pipe sections form a shared fuel network.
Storage tanks connected to the same network contribute to its capacity.

============================================================
3. ROOMS AND AIRPORT MANAGEMENT
============================================================

You can assign a purpose to a detected room.
The room information panel shows its requirements and whether it is functional.

CEO OFFICE
A CEO Office needs:
- At least one office desk.
- At least one chair.
- At least one computer.
- At least one printer.
- At least one door.

A valid CEO Office makes the airport operational.
New scheduled aircraft arrivals and new hobby aircraft arrivals require it.
If the office becomes invalid, new arrivals are disabled.
Aircraft already operating are handled by their own flight systems.

CFO OFFICE
A CFO Office needs the same basic office furniture:
- At least one office desk.
- At least one chair.
- At least one computer.
- At least one printer.
- At least one door.

A valid CFO Office unlocks custom airport fees.
Without a valid CFO Office, aircraft use their default fee profiles.

FLIGHT SCHOOL
A Flight School needs:
- At least 20 foundation cells.
- At least one door.
- At least one instructor desk.
- At least one chair.

The room's quality depends on its area, student seats and instructor desks.

Area quality:
- Fewer than 20 cells: 0 points.
- 20–29 cells: 10 points.
- 30–39 cells: 20 points.
- 40–59 cells: 30 points.
- 60 or more cells: 40 points.

Student seating quality:
- No seats: 0 points.
- 1 seat: 10 points.
- 2–3 seats: 20 points.
- 4–5 seats: 30 points.
- 6 or more seats: 40 points.

Instructor desk quality:
- No instructor desk: 0 points.
- 1 instructor desk: 10 points.
- 2 or more instructor desks: 20 points.

Facility Quality Score = Area Points + Seating Points + Desk Points.

The score determines daily student demand:
- Below 20 points: 0 students per day.
- 20–39 points: up to 2 students per day.
- 40–59 points: up to 4 students per day.
- 60–79 points: up to 6 students per day.
- 80 points or more: up to 8 students per day.

Daily demand cannot exceed the number of student seats.
Flight School operates from 08:00 until 18:00.
A training aircraft waits 30 game minutes before its first lesson.
It also waits 30 game minutes between completed lessons.
A lesson consists of five training circuits.
Each completed lesson rewards the airport with $300.

PILOTS LOUNGE
A Pilots Lounge needs a detected room and at least one door.
You can choose how it affects the room's environment:
- Additive mode adds an environment bonus. The default bonus is 25 points.
- Set To 100 mode sets the environment score inside the lounge to 100.

The lounge effect applies only inside the assigned room.
The room must remain enclosed and have a valid door.

============================================================
4. AIRCRAFT AND FLIGHT OPERATIONS
============================================================

The airport supports several aircraft operation types.
These include hobby aircraft, training aircraft, sightseeing aircraft, cargo aircraft and passenger aircraft.

SCHEDULED FLIGHTS
Scheduled flights use the airport flight schedule.
A flight has a flight number, an arrival time and a gate assignment.
The airport checks that the assigned gate is suitable and reachable.
If a suitable route or gate is not available, the aircraft waits for another attempt.
A valid CEO Office is required for new scheduled arrivals.

LANDING AND TAKEOFF
Aircraft select a suitable runway based on weather and traffic.
Wind direction affects headwind and crosswind.
The systems prefer runways with acceptable crosswind.
Traffic and route availability also influence runway selection.

The default maximum permitted crosswind component is 15 knots.
If crosswind is too strong during the landing flare, the aircraft can go around.
A go-around means the aircraft climbs away and tries again later.

Aircraft taxi between runways, taxiways, parking areas and gates.
The ground traffic controller monitors shared taxiway sections and intersections.
Aircraft may be held at a holding point until the route is clear.
Runway reservations prevent conflicting landings, line-ups and departures.
A runway remains occupied until the aircraft has physically cleared it.

HOBBY AIRCRAFT VISITORS
Hobby aircraft arrive according to airport demand.
The airport checks for a possible visitor every 15 game minutes by default.
A free grass parking spot and a usable runway are required.
The number of active hobby aircraft is limited by the spawner setting.

The base expected daily demand is calculated as:

Base Demand =
1.5
+ (0.85 × number of grass parking spots)
+ (0.5 × number of runways beyond the first)

The result is capped at 15 expected visits per day before demand modifiers.

Demand is then influenced by:
- Airport rating: 0 rating gives 0.5x demand; 100 rating gives 2x.
- Time of day: daytime 1.0x, dawn/dusk 0.5x, night 0.1x.
- Weekend: 1.25x by default.
- Available aircraft fuel station: 1.5x by default.
- Landing and parking fees: higher fees reduce demand.

The reputation multiplier is:
0.5 + (Airport Rating / 100 × 1.5).

The fee multiplier is:
1 / (1 + landing fee × 0.001 + parking fee × 0.001).

The final expected daily demand is divided by the number of demand checks per day.
That gives the probability of an arrival at each check.
The probability is limited to a maximum of 100%.

When a hobby aircraft parks, its visit timer starts.
The default visit lasts between 60 and 180 game minutes.
The pilot appears five game minutes after parking.
When the visit ends, the pilot returns to the aircraft.
The pilot walks around the aircraft before boarding.
After boarding, the aircraft waits another 15 game minutes before departure.
The parking spot is released after the aircraft moves away.

SIGHTSEEING FLIGHTS
A sightseeing ticket desk is required.
The airport schedules one sightseeing flight at each new game-hour by default.
If no parking spot is available, the pending flight retries.
Missed hourly slots do not build up into multiple flights.

The default aircraft capacity is six passengers.
The default ticket price is $100.
Reference demand is four passengers at that price.
Demand changes inversely with ticket price and varies randomly by up to 25%.
Passenger demand cannot exceed aircraft capacity.

Expected passenger demand is:
Reference Demand × (100 / Ticket Price),
then adjusted by the random variation and limited by aircraft capacity.

The airport receives a 20% commission on sightseeing ticket revenue by default.
The commission is paid after a successful takeoff.

PASSENGER FLIGHTS
Passenger flights use the scheduled flight system.
The default base ticket price is $10.
An airport fee can be configured as a percentage of the base fare.
Revenue is recorded after a successful departure.

Ticket Revenue =
Passengers × Base Ticket Price × (1 + Airport Fee Percentage / 100).

The current passenger systems handle aircraft passenger counts, boarding revenue and baggage.
Independent passenger needs and passenger AI are not yet integrated.

CARGO FLIGHTS
Cargo flights use the scheduled flight and gate systems.
Cargo vehicles transfer baggage between aircraft and cargo zones.
Incoming baggage is unloaded from the aircraft.
Outgoing baggage is collected from cargo zones and loaded onto the aircraft.
Packages are reserved during transfer to prevent duplicate handling.
Cargo-only flights skip passenger deboarding, cleaning and boarding tasks.
They still require applicable fueling and baggage services.

============================================================
5. AIRCRAFT TURNAROUND AND AIRPORT SERVICES
============================================================

A parked aircraft begins its turnaround process.
Depending on its operation type, the turnaround can include:
- Passenger deboarding.
- Baggage unloading.
- Aircraft cleaning.
- Fueling.
- Baggage loading.
- Passenger boarding.

Some tasks use timed game-time durations.
Other tasks are completed by airport service vehicles.
The aircraft becomes ready to depart when its required tasks are complete.

FUEL
Aircraft consume fuel while flying.
The default aircraft fuel consumption is 40 litres per in-game flight hour.
Each aircraft has its own fuel tank capacity.

Airport fuel trucks can refuel parked aircraft.
The truck travels to the aircraft, transfers fuel and charges for the service.
The truck returns to its refill station when required.
The truck's own fuel supply is limited.

Fuel delivery tankers bring fuel to airport fuel stations.
Fuel pipes connect stations and storage tanks into shared networks.
Each connected network has one shared fuel reserve.
Connected storage tanks contribute to network capacity.
Automatic tanker delivery is requested when a network's fuel falls to 25% of capacity by default.

CLEANING AND WASTE
Cleaning vehicles travel to parked aircraft.
They perform the cleaning process and return to their home lot.
Cleaning generates waste.
The cleaning fee depends on aircraft passenger capacity.

Waste is stored in airport waste disposal objects.
A garbage collector is requested when a disposal reaches 75% full by default.
A collector can carry up to 500 waste units by default.
It collects waste over time and then leaves the airport.

Waste collection cost is:
$25 fixed trip fee + $0.20 per collected waste unit.
The final charge is rounded up to the next whole currency unit.

CARGO VEHICLES
Cargo tugs move packages between aircraft and cargo zones.
A tug can collect outgoing packages, carry them and deliver them to the aircraft.
Incoming packages are moved from aircraft to a cargo zone.
Vehicle routes use the airport road and traffic systems.

============================================================
6. HOBBY PILOT BEHAVIOUR
============================================================

Each hobby pilot has eight needs.
Need values range from 0 to 100.
100 means fully satisfied.
0 means critical.

The eight needs are:
- Thirst.
- Hunger.
- Bladder.
- Energy.
- Social.
- Entertainment.
- Comfort.
- Boredom.

Pilots begin each visit with independently randomised needs.
The current pilot prefab uses a starting range of 50–100 for each need.

Needs decrease over game time.
Default decay per game minute is:
- Thirst: 0.25.
- Hunger: 0.15.
- Bladder: 0.20.
- Energy: 0.08.
- Social: 0.06.
- Entertainment: 0.08.
- Comfort: 0.05 before environmental adjustment.
- Boredom: 0.05.

The pilot AI checks available interactions.
It estimates how useful each interaction would be.
Urgent needs receive more influence.
Each need has its own urgency curve and importance weight.
Interactions can also be penalised when they conflict with another urgent need.
Travel time, queue waiting time and interaction duration reduce the final score.

The pilot considers a need-based interaction only while the relevant need is below its configured threshold.
The default threshold for the listed interactions is 80.
The pilot selects from its best candidates using weighted random choice.
It does not always choose the single highest score.

INTERACTIONS
Current interaction examples include:
- Drink: restores 30 Thirst in 5 seconds.
- Buy Drink: restores 20 Thirst in 5 seconds.
- Buy Food: restores 30 Hunger in 8 seconds.
- Use Toilet: restores 50 Bladder in 10 seconds.
- Sit / Rest: restores 25 Energy and 15 Comfort in 20 seconds.
- Watch TV: restores 30 Entertainment and 20 Boredom in 30 seconds.
- Watch Aircraft: restores 30 Entertainment and 20 Boredom in 180 seconds.

An interaction object can limit how many pilots use it at once.
Some objects have a waiting queue.
The first pilot reserves the interaction.
Other pilots wait in line and are promoted when the interaction becomes available.

SOCIAL ACTIVITIES
Two pilots can start a conversation when both can participate.
They prefer a pair of available seats when possible.
If suitable seats are unavailable, they can talk while standing.

A standing conversation lasts four game minutes by default.
It restores 15 Social, 10 Entertainment and 10 Boredom to each pilot.

A seated conversation lasts ten game minutes by default.
It restores 25 Social, 10 Comfort, 15 Entertainment and 15 Boredom.

Groups of three to five pilots can have a group conversation.
The group prefers seats when enough are available.
Otherwise, the pilots gather in a standing circle.
A group conversation lasts 15 game minutes.
It restores 35 Social, 20 Entertainment and 20 Boredom.

WANDERING
Pilots wander when no higher-priority activity is available.
They use reachable foundation areas for their wandering destinations.
They pause between walks.
Pilots can walk on grass, foundations and tarmac.
They avoid buildings, walls, runways and taxiways.
They also try to avoid bumping into one another.

Pilots prefer destinations inside a Pilots Lounge.
The default lounge destination weight is 2x.
A pilot already inside the lounge gets an additional 2x stay weight.
This makes lounge destinations more likely, but does not force pilots to stay there.

When an aircraft is ready to depart, its pilot stops other activities.
The pilot walks around the aircraft, returns to the boarding position and boards.
The pilot then disappears from the airport.

============================================================
7. ENVIRONMENT AND COMFORT
============================================================

The airport has a local environment score from 0 to 100.
The neutral starting score is 50.
Placed objects can improve or reduce the score.
Each object has an environment value and an influence radius.

The object's influence decreases linearly with distance.
At the object, the full environment value applies.
At the edge of its radius, the contribution is zero.
Objects outside their radius have no effect.

Object Contribution =
Environment Value × (1 - Distance / Influence Radius).

Contributions from nearby objects are added together.
The final score is limited to the range 0–100.
Distance is measured horizontally across the ground.

A Pilots Lounge can add its configured bonus or set the score to 100.
Its room effect applies only to positions inside the lounge.

Environment affects pilot Comfort decay.
A poor environment makes Comfort decrease faster.
A good environment slows Comfort decay.

The default Comfort decay multiplier is:
- Environment 0: 2x normal decay.
- Environment 50: 1x normal decay.
- Environment 100: 0x normal decay.

The multiplier changes linearly between these values.
The original Comfort decay rate is 0.05 per game minute.

A high environment score does not directly add Comfort points.
It helps pilots keep their existing Comfort for longer.

============================================================
8. AIRPORT RATING AND REPUTATION
============================================================

The airport rating ranges from 0 to 100.
It is based on visitor satisfaction and the environment experienced by visitors.

Visitor Satisfaction is the average value of the visitor's eight needs.
Environment Experience is the environment score experienced during the visit.

The rating uses time-weighted visitor experience.
A visitor who stays longer contributes more than a visitor who stays briefly.

The default rating formula is:
Airport Rating =
(Visitor Satisfaction × 0.75) +
(Environment Experience × 0.25).

The displayed rating moves gradually toward the calculated rating.
The default smoothing time is 30 game seconds.

Hobby aircraft demand uses airport rating as a reputation multiplier.
A rating of 0 gives 0.5x demand.
A rating of 50 gives 1x demand.
A rating of 100 gives 2x demand.

The current needs-based visitor experience system is active for hobby pilots.
Passenger visitor AI and passenger needs are planned for later development.

============================================================
9. AIRPORT FINANCES
============================================================

A new airport starts with $50,000 by default.
Building placement spends money.
Aircraft services and airport operations can generate income or costs.

Aircraft can pay:
- Landing fees.
- Recurring parking fees.
- Fuel service fees.
- Cleaning fees.

Landing fees are charged after a completed landing.
Parking fees are charged at configured game-time intervals.
The fee profile depends on aircraft operation type.

A valid CFO Office unlocks custom fee settings.
Without one, aircraft use their default fee profiles.

Default fee profiles are:
- Hobby aircraft: $20 landing, $5 parking every 10 game minutes.
- Flight School aircraft: $20 landing, $5 parking every 10 game minutes.
- Sightseeing aircraft: $250 landing, $100 parking every 15 game minutes.
- Cargo aircraft: $250 landing, $100 parking every 15 game minutes.
- Passenger aircraft: $250 landing, $100 parking every 15 game minutes.

Cleaning fees depend on aircraft seat capacity.
Default fee tiers are:
- Up to 10 seats: $100.
- Up to 50 seats: $200.
- Up to 100 seats: $350.
- Up to 200 seats: $500.
- Up to 300 seats: $750.
- More than 300 seats: $1,000.

Sightseeing flights generate ticket revenue.
The airport receives its configured commission.
Passenger flights record ticket sales and airport fees after successful departure.
Flight School pays a reward for each completed lesson.
Fuel delivery, aircraft services and waste collection create operating costs.

============================================================
10. SAVING YOUR AIRPORT
============================================================

Save slots store the airport layout and its main management data.
A save includes placed buildings, foundations, tarmac, roads and taxiways.
It also stores pipelines and their fuel reserves.
Room-purpose assignments and the flight schedule are saved.
Game time, simulation speed and airport money are saved.
Floor and wall finishes are saved with the airport layout.

Starting a new game clears the current airport and resets its starting money and clock.
Existing save slots are not deleted when a new game is started.

The airport rating and active visitor experience are currently session systems.
They are not included in the airport save data.
